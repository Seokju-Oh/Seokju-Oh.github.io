Seokju Oh


I am a PhD Candidate in the History Department at Columbia University, specializing in the political economy and foreign relations of the US in the long twentieth century.

My dissertation examines the transformation of the US national state and the rise of US global supremacy during the era of the world wars (1917-1946). Drawing primarily on archival materials from sixteen US states and DC, it demonstrates how sectionalism in the nation's food- and raw material-producing regions in the South and West posed a formidable domestic constraint on the project of US global supremacy. It traces how the project's advocates intially sought to circumvent these sectional challenges through ad-hoc measures, and later attempted to resolve them through policy "fixes" aimed at incorporating broader regional interests. In doing so, they fundamentally reshaped both the political institutions and the economic geography of the nation.

Ultimatey, my research aims to challenge dominant accounts of American political development by foregrounding the most basic yet often overlooked reality of the polity: the US as a transcontinental federation marked by regional variation.

In AY 25-26, I will be in residence at the Harvard Kennedy School as an Ernest May Fellow in History & Policy.
